The first pot of the day is tea, and it is already on the stove before anyone is properly awake. Bread from yesterday is warmed on the flat pan, olive oil goes into a saucer, and somebody cracks eggs into a small clay dish with a pinch of cumin and salt. Breakfast in Morocco is slow and warm, and it is never in a hurry to end.

Lunch is the real meal. Shops close, children come home, and the big dish goes in the middle of the table: a tagine, a couscous on Friday, a stew of whatever the market had that morning. This is where the serious spices live, the cumin and ginger, the paprika, the long list inside a good ras el hanout.

By evening the kitchen softens. A bowl of soup, something sweet with the tea, cinnamon dusted over almonds, a pinch of saffron in a pot that has been simmering for hours.

Three meals, three moods. We built the shelf the same way: [[golden-hour]] for the first light, [[ras-el-hanout]] for the long lunch, [[sweet-tagine]] for the evening. Cook with the hour and the day sorts itself out.

The tagine is already half gone when the lid comes back on. Someone tears another piece of bread, leans in, and pulls the best of the meat towards the guest. You said you were full. Nobody heard you.

This is how a Moroccan table works. Food is not portioned, it is shared, and the host's job is to make sure no one leaves it with an empty corner of their stomach. Refusing is part of the game. Giving in is the point.

We started Spice Hanout with that second helping in mind. Not a diet, not a plan, not a list of things you should stop eating. A kitchen that smells of cumin at noon and cinnamon at dusk, and a table you stay at a little longer than you meant to.

Plenty of people grew up being told to eat less, to leave something on the plate, to earn dessert. We would rather you slowed down. Eat at the hour your body asks for food, with people you like, from a dish everyone reaches into. Taste what is in it. Then have some more.

That is the whole idea. The spices are only there to make the second helping impossible to refuse. Start with [[ras-el-hanout]], or with [[a-whole-day|the box]] if you want the whole day at once.

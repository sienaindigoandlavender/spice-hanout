The round loaf arrives warm, wrapped in a cloth, and is torn rather than cut. Everyone gets a piece. Nobody gets a fork.

At a Moroccan table bread does the work cutlery does elsewhere. You pinch it around a piece of meat, scoop a little sauce, chase a chickpea across the dish. Three fingers of the right hand, never the whole palm. Children learn it before they can read.

And when the meat is gone, the best part begins. The sauce at the bottom of the tagine, thick with oil, onion and spice, is the cook's real signature. Leaving it would be an insult. So the bread goes round the plate until the clay shows through.

That last wipe is the reason we care so much about what goes into a sauce. A good [[chermoula]] or a pinch of [[kemoun]] is not decoration. It is the taste that is still on your fingers when the table is cleared.

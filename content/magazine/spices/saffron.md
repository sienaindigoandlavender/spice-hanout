Before dawn in Taliouine, families walk the fields bent double, picking purple crocus flowers into baskets. They have a few hours. Once the sun opens the petals, the flowers wilt fast and the work gets harder.

Back at the house the flowers are tipped onto a table and everyone sits round it, pinching out the three red threads from each one. It takes about 150 flowers to make a single gram. That is why saffron is weighed like gold and sold in tiny tins.

Taliouine, high in the Anti-Atlas between Taroudant and Ouarzazate, is the heart of Moroccan saffron, and the harvest runs for a few weeks from late October. The town holds a saffron festival when it is done.

In the kitchen a little goes a long way. A pinch, crumbled and soaked in a spoon of warm water, turns a tagine golden and gives it that faint honeyed bitterness nothing else has. We sell [[taliouine-saffron|the threads]] on their own, and put a careful pinch into [[sweet-tagine]].

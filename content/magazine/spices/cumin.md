At a grill stall in Jemaa el-Fna there are two small dishes on every table: one of salt, one of ground cumin. Nobody explains them. You dip.

Cumin is the most Moroccan of spices. It goes on boiled eggs at breakfast, into the carrot salad at lunch, over grilled liver and slow-roasted lamb, into the chickpeas of a bowl of harira. Mixed with salt it is simply called *kamoun*, and it lives next to the salt cellar in most kitchens.

The best is said to come from the southeast, around Alnif, where the seed is small and the smell sharp. Toasting it for a minute in a dry pan before grinding wakes it up: it turns warmer, deeper, a little smoky.

Our [[kemoun]] is exactly that: toasted cumin and sea salt, nothing else. It also sits at the heart of our [[ras-el-hanout]] and our [[chermoula]].

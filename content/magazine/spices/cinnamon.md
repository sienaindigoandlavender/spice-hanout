The pastilla comes out of the oven, crisp and golden, and the last thing the cook does before it goes to the table is the most dramatic: a snowfall of icing sugar, then cinnamon drawn across it in a lattice of thin brown lines.

Cinnamon is how Moroccan kitchens say "this is the sweet part". It goes over seffa, the sweet buttered vermicelli with almonds, into tagines of lamb with prunes or quince, and into the milk at breakfast.

There are two kinds in the souk. Cassia is the thick, dark bark, strong and a little fiery. Ceylon cinnamon rolls up in thin, papery layers and tastes softer and sweeter. We use Ceylon, because it lets the other spices speak.

You will find it in [[sweet-tagine]] for the evening, [[spiced-coffee]] for the morning cup, and [[golden-hour]] in warm milk.
